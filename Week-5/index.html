1. What changes when sensors form a network?

A single sensor measures one location. A sensor network combines multiple sensing nodes that communicate to monitor a larger area or several locations.

Sensor nodes→Wireless links / forwarding→Gateway / sink→Collected readings
Sensing nodeMeasures a condition and sends data. It may also forward other nodes' data.
LinkConnection used to transfer packets between nodes.
Gateway / sinkCollects network readings and may connect to other systems.
RoutingChooses a path for packets, especially when data travels through several nodes.

2. Interactive topology explorer

Choose a topology and trace how a reading reaches the gateway. Star uses direct links; multi-hop allows nodes to forward packets through neighbors.

3. The four network-design trade-offs

📍 CoverageAre all required places or objects within sensing and communication reach?
🔋 EnergyBattery-powered nodes must limit sensing, processing and radio activity.
📡 ReliabilityObstacles, interference and failed links can prevent readings from arriving.
⏱️ Reporting frequencyMore frequent updates improve timeliness but may increase traffic and energy use.

4. Design challenge: university garden

Several soil-moisture nodes are spread across a garden. Change the conditions and choose a topology.

Illustrative assumptions for this activity: a direct link is considered reliable up to 80 m in clear conditions and 50 m with obstacles. These are teaching values, not radio specifications.
